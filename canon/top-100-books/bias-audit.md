## Findings

Severity follows docs/architecture: high blocks the proposal, medium
and low are recorded for the reviewer. No finding is high.

1. **Geographic bias, medium.** Europe and North America hold 36 of
   100 places, concentrated in the modern novel and in modern
   philosophy. This reflects the real reach of those works since
   1800, but the reviewer should test whether modern works from
   South and Southeast Asia, Africa and the Arabic-speaking world
   were scored on the same terms.
2. **Home-tradition check, medium.** Islamic civilization holds 19
   places. Docs/03 says the library's emphasis on Islamic
   civilization affects coverage, not rankings, so each of these was
   scored only on the six criteria. Most rank on transmission that is
   well attested outside the tradition (algebra, medicine, optics,
   the Nights, Persian poetry). The reviewer should confirm the scores
   of the works whose reach stayed mostly within the tradition
   (*al-Risala*, *Sirat Rasul Allah*, the *Divan* of Ghalib).
3. **Anglophone bias, low.** 15 works were written in English, none in
   the top ten. Translation availability was not used as a proxy for
   quality.
4. **Language bias, low.** Translation limits most affect poetry.
   Poetry in Persian, Arabic, Urdu, Chinese, Tamil and Vietnamese was
   scored for achievement within its own language, per docs/03.
5. **Religious bias, low.** Scriptures and religious works of
   Islam, Judaism, Christianity, Hinduism, Buddhism, Confucianism and
   Daoism are ranked by influence and endurance, not by belief.
   Zoroastrian, Sikh and Jain scriptures are not on the list; the
   Religious Texts Canon is the place to cover them in depth.
6. **Recency bias, low.** The latest work is from 1987. Works under
   about fifty years old cannot yet be scored fairly on endurance.
7. **Colonial and indigenous gaps, medium.** Indigenous traditions
   and Africa hold 3 places. Oral traditions are
   underrepresented because the canon ranks books; the Epic of
   Sundiata and the Popol Vuh stand for traditions that deserve a
   fuller domain canon.
8. **Gender, medium.** 5 works are by women. This follows
   historical exclusion from writing and publication rather than the
   criteria, but it should be revisited at each annual review.
