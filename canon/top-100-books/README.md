# Top 100 Greatest Books Ever Written

Version 1.0, draft of 2026-10-13. Status: awaiting the owner's G4
approval.

## Purpose

This canon names the hundred books that have most shaped human
thought, faith, law, science and imagination, across every era and
civilization. It is the entry point to the library's reading: each
work here will gain a summary, study notes and a place in the
reading paths as the library grows.

## How the list was made

The Canon Editor agent applied the
[Global Canon Methodology](../../docs/03-global-canon-methodology.md)
through its `canon-ranking` skill, with the `representation-check`,
`comparative-analysis`, `influence-analysis` and `bias-audit` skills
it depends on. Every work was scored from 1 to 10 on the six weighted
criteria, giving a total out of 100:

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Civilizational influence | 25% |
| Literary and intellectual achievement | 20% |
| Endurance across time | 15% |
| Global reach and cross-cultural influence | 15% |
| Importance within its own tradition | 15% |
| Scholarly consensus and historical legacy | 10% |

Ties are broken in the methodology's order: civilizational influence,
then endurance, then global reach, then scholarly consensus. Works
still level after that are listed by title. That last rule decides
the top two places: the Hebrew Bible and the Quran are tied on every
criterion, so their order carries no meaning beyond the alphabet.

Scope. "Ever written" is read as the whole of world literature and
thought: scripture, epic, philosophy, history, science and the novel
all compete on the same criteria, as docs/03 requires. Religious
texts are assessed as historical and civilizational works; their
scores make no judgement on belief. A multi-volume or collected work
counts as one book.

## Directory structure

```text
canon/top-100-books/
├── README.md        the ranked list (this page)
├── scoring.md       per-criterion scores and a rationale for each work
├── bias-audit.md    representation report and bias audit
└── CHANGELOG.md     version history
```

Machine-readable records live in `metadata/`: the canon itself in
`metadata/canons.yaml` (`canon-top-100-books`), the works in
`metadata/books.yaml`, and their authors and poets in
`metadata/authors.yaml` and `metadata/poets.yaml`.
