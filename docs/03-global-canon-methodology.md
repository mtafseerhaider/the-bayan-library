# Global Canon Methodology

## Purpose

The Global Canon Methodology defines how The Bayan Library evaluates, ranks,
maintains, and revises lists of the greatest books, authors, poets, thinkers,
and intellectual works in human history.

The objective is to establish a globally representative canon that reflects
humanity's shared intellectual heritage while avoiding unnecessary cultural,
linguistic, religious, geographic, or ideological bias.

## Scope

This methodology governs:

- Top 100 Greatest Books Ever Written
- Top 500 Books Canon
- World Literature Canon
- Islamic Civilization Canon
- Poetry Canon
- Philosophy Canon
- Religious Texts Canon
- Political Thought Canon
- Scientific Works Canon

Specialized canons may extend this methodology but shall not contradict it.

## Canon Definition

A canon is a curated collection of works considered exceptionally significant
due to their influence, quality, historical importance, or enduring relevance.

A canon is not:

- A bestseller list
- A popularity ranking
- A personal favorites list
- A social media recommendation list

A canon is an attempt to identify works of lasting significance.

## Core Principle

The canon should represent humanity's greatest intellectual achievements rather
than the achievements of any single civilization.

No civilization shall be treated as the default benchmark.

Every work should first be evaluated within its own historical and cultural
context before being compared globally.

## Evaluation Framework

Each work should be evaluated using six weighted criteria.

## Civilizational Influence (25%)

Measure:

- Impact on civilization
- Influence on institutions
- Influence on religion
- Influence on philosophy
- Influence on ethics
- Influence on politics
- Influence on science
- Influence on culture

Guiding question:

How much would human civilization differ if this work had never existed?

## Literary and Intellectual Achievement (20%)

Measure:

- Originality
- Complexity
- Craftsmanship
- Narrative quality
- Philosophical depth
- Intellectual innovation
- Expressive power

Works should be judged according to the standards of their tradition and
historical period.

## Endurance Across Time (15%)

Measure:

- Longevity
- Survival across generations
- Continuing influence
- Continuing readership
- Ongoing relevance

Preference should be given to works that continue to matter long after their
creation.

## Global Reach and Cross-Cultural Influence (15%)

Measure:

- Translation history
- International readership
- Geographic influence
- Cultural transmission
- Impact beyond original language

A work's ability to influence multiple civilizations is a significant factor.

## Importance Within Its Own Tradition (15%)

Measure:

- Canonical status
- Scholarly recognition
- Cultural significance
- Regional influence
- Foundational importance

A work may receive a high score even if it remains relatively unknown outside
its originating culture.

## Scholarly Consensus and Historical Legacy (10%)

Measure:

- Academic recognition
- Scholarly discussion
- University inclusion
- Canon appearances
- Historical reputation

Scholarly approval alone is insufficient but remains important.

## Representation Principles

The canon must seek meaningful representation from major intellectual
traditions.

These include:

- Mesopotamia
- Egypt
- Greece
- Rome
- Persia
- Islamic Civilization
- India
- China
- Japan
- Africa
- Indigenous Traditions
- Europe
- Latin America
- Modern Global Literature

Representation does not guarantee inclusion.

Inclusion must remain merit-based.

## Eligible Genres

All forms of significant intellectual expression are eligible.

Examples include:

- Epic poetry
- Novels
- Drama
- Philosophy
- Religious texts
- Theology
- Mysticism
- History
- Political theory
- Scientific works
- Essays
- Literary criticism

No genre receives automatic priority over another.

## Religious Text Evaluation

Religious texts may be evaluated as historical, intellectual, literary, and
civilizational works.

Evaluation should consider:

- Historical influence
- Cultural impact
- Intellectual significance
- Literary characteristics
- Continuing relevance

Evaluation should not depend on agreement or disagreement with the beliefs
contained within the text.

## Philosophy Evaluation

Philosophical works should be assessed using:

- Originality
- Argument quality
- Historical influence
- Intellectual depth
- Continuing relevance

Influence and insight should both be considered.

## Poetry Evaluation

Poetry should be evaluated according to:

- Literary quality
- Emotional power
- Philosophical depth
- Linguistic achievement
- Influence
- Cultural significance
- Historical endurance

Translation limitations should be considered when comparing works across
languages.

## Islamic Civilization Representation

The Bayan Library places special emphasis on preserving and studying Islamic
intellectual history.

Special attention should be given to:

- Philosophy
- Theology
- Mysticism
- Literature
- Science
- History
- Political thought

This emphasis affects coverage and documentation depth.

It does not automatically affect rankings.

All rankings must continue to follow the established methodology.

## Persianate Civilization Representation

Special attention should be given to:

- Persian literature
- Persian philosophy
- Persian poetry
- Persian intellectual history

Major figures include:

- Ferdowsi
- Saadi
- Rumi
- Hafez
- Attar
- Jami
- Mulla Sadra
- Allamah Tabataba'i

Their works should receive comprehensive coverage.

## Urdu Literature Representation

The repository should preserve and document major Urdu literary achievements.

Priority figures include:

- Mir Taqi Mir
- Mirza Ghalib
- Muhammad Iqbal
- Faiz Ahmed Faiz
- Ahmad Faraz
- Jaun Elia
- Parveen Shakir

Coverage should include biography, major works, themes, and influence.

## Bias Audit Requirements

Every major ranking update should include a bias review.

The review should assess:

- Geographic bias
- Language bias
- Religious bias
- Cultural bias
- Colonial bias
- Anglophone bias
- Recency bias
- Genre bias

Results should be documented.

## Ranking Process

The ranking process consists of:

1. Candidate identification
2. Preliminary evaluation
3. Comparative analysis
4. Scoring
5. Representation review
6. Bias audit
7. Editorial review
8. Publication

Each step should be documented when possible.

## Tie-Breaking Rules

When two works receive equivalent evaluations:

Priority should be given to the work with:

1. Greater civilizational influence
2. Greater historical longevity
3. Wider cross-cultural impact
4. Stronger scholarly consensus

Tie-breaking should be explained whenever applied.

## Canon Revision Policy

The canon is not static.

Revisions may occur when:

- New scholarship emerges
- Historical understanding improves
- Representation issues are identified
- Methodological improvements are adopted

Changes should be documented with rationale.

## Canon Stability Principle

Frequent dramatic changes should be avoided.

Canon revisions should prioritize:

- Stability
- Transparency
- Scholarly justification

A canon should evolve gradually rather than fluctuate unpredictably.

## Documentation Requirements

Every ranked work should include:

- Author
- Date or period
- Civilization or region
- Genre
- Justification
- Influence summary
- Related works

Metadata should remain consistent across the repository.

## Success Criteria

A successful canon should be:

- Globally representative
- Academically defensible
- Historically informed
- Methodologically transparent
- Useful to readers
- Sustainable to maintain

The goal is not perfection.

The goal is the most rigorous and intellectually honest canon possible.

## Methodology Summary

The Global Canon Methodology exists to identify humanity's most significant
intellectual achievements while respecting the diversity of civilizations,
traditions, languages, and forms of knowledge.

Every ranking should help readers better understand the ideas that shaped human
history.
